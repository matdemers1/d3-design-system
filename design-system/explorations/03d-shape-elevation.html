3d — Shape & elevation

Five radii, one border weight, no shadows anywhere, and a focus ring proved on every surface it can land on.

This is the sub-phase that has to make good on D-015. Quiet was locked partly because it has no shadows at all — depth is tone, and a surface that is nearer is a step lighter. That is easy for a card and hard for the five components that conventionally reach for a shadow: menu, popover, tooltip, toast and modal. It is hardest in light mode, where the tonal ladder ends at white and there is nowhere lighter to go. The answer below is one added rule, not an exception.

The elevation model

Elevation is tone. Detachment is a boundary.

Resting surfaces — the shell, the page, a card, a hovered row — differ by tone alone and carry no border. Floating layers — menu, popover, tooltip, toast, modal — sit at surface-raised and carry a 1px border-float at 3:1. A modal adds a scrim; nothing else does.

The boundary is not decoration and it is not a shadow in disguise. It exists because in light mode the tonal ladder terminates at white: a menu opening over a white card has no lighter step available. Rather than run two different elevation mechanisms in the two modes, the boundary applies in both — which also makes a menu legible when it opens inside a modal, where tone has already been spent.

A correction from 3a. The first mapping had surface-raised at neutral-800 and surface-hover at neutral-700 — which meant a floating panel was darker than a hovered row, so a tooltip over a hovered row sat below it tonally. In a system where tone is the only depth cue that is not a nitpick, it is the mechanism failing. The two are now swapped and the ladder is monotonic. 124 pairs re-validated, zero failures.

Radius five steps

--rad-xs6pxcheckbox, keyboard hint, badge on an icon
--rad-sm8pxchip, status pill on a square corner, small control
--rad-md10pxbutton, input, select, menu item, tab
--rad-lg14pxcard, table container, modal, menu panel
--rad-full999pxpill, avatar, switch, vote count
Borders have one weight: 1px. There is no 2px border anywhere in the system. Selection is a fill (accent-muted), not a thicker outline; the focus ring is an outline, not a border. Two roles, one weight: border is a decorative divider with no contrast requirement, and border-field / border-float are findable boundaries held to 3:1.

Why an outline and not a box-shadow ring. The 2px offset reveals the surface behind the element, which is what separates the ring from a filled control — a violet ring drawn tight against a violet button would vanish. Using outline also keeps the no-shadow rule literal rather than technically-honoured-via-box-shadow. And it is :focus-visible, never :focus — the distinction Bindery and App D both got right, and the reason keyboard focus styling survives contact with mouse users.
